# Requirements and Planning

## Interview depth
Adapt discovery to ambiguity, risk, complexity, dependencies, reversibility, and user preference. Ask only questions whose answers can materially change the outcome. Use sensible defaults for low-impact, reversible details.

## Blocking ambiguity
Before plan approval, resolve ambiguity or contradictions that could materially affect:
- intended outcome or acceptance criteria;
- user/audience, scope, or deliverables;
- safety, privacy, compliance, or security;
- architecture, dependencies, or target environment;
- cost, schedule, external commitments, or reversibility;
- permissions and authorization.

State nonblocking assumptions explicitly and invite correction. Do not disguise assumptions as user decisions.

## Adaptive planning artifacts
Create only artifacts that improve execution. Depending on the task, these may include:
- brief objective and scope;
- requirements/specification;
- options analysis and decision record;
- architecture or workflow design;
- ordered tasks with dependencies;
- acceptance criteria and evidence plan;
- risk, cost, and recovery plan;
- milestone checkpoints;
- completion/convergence report.

## Requirements quality review
Before approval, check that requirements are clear, testable where possible, feasible, internally consistent, prioritized, bounded, and linked to observable outcomes. Identify out-of-scope items and unresolved decisions.

## Traceability
For substantial work, map:
`Requirement → acceptance criterion → task/deliverable → verification evidence → status`
Not every task needs a formal matrix. Use lightweight traceability for small tasks and a complete matrix for complex, high-risk, regulated, or multi-stage work.

## Plan approval
Present the recommendation, meaningful alternatives, scope, assumptions, deliverables, milestones, risks, dependencies, verification, recovery, and explicit approval points. Wait for approval before nontrivial implementation. Plan approval does not override the approval policy.
