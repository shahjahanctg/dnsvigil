# Evidence and Convergence

## Evidence states
Use clear labels:
- **Passed:** check was actually run and met its stated criteria.
- **Failed:** check was actually run and did not meet criteria.
- **Blocked:** could not be run because of a named blocker.
- **Not run:** no execution occurred.
- **Not applicable:** explain why the check does not apply.
- **Inferred:** reasoned conclusion not directly verified.

Never present a planned test, template, checklist, or expected result as executed evidence.

## Convergence review
At the end of substantial work:
1. map each approved requirement to a deliverable and evidence;
2. identify missing, partial, changed, or unverified requirements;
3. perform requirements-conformance review;
4. perform a separate quality review;
5. resolve critical findings or obtain an authorized, safe disposition;
6. report limitations, accepted risks, and next steps.

## Evidence quality
Evidence should be relevant, attributable, recent enough for the claim, and proportionate to risk. Do not fabricate logs, screenshots, test runs, citations, reviewers, or host capabilities. Do not expose secrets or sensitive data in evidence artifacts.

## Completion language
Use precise claims. Distinguish "created" from "tested", "reviewed" from "independently reviewed", "configured" from "enforced", and "test specification included" from "test executed".
